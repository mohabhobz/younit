A price chart is a record of what happened. Nothing more, nothing less. Every candlestick is four numbers: where the price opened, where it closed, the highest it reached, the lowest it reached. Volume tells you how many shares changed hands.

Learning to read charts is not about predicting the future. It's about understanding the past well enough to ask the right questions.

## OHLCV: the five numbers

Every trading day produces five numbers:

- **O** — Open: the first transaction price of the day
- **H** — High: the highest price traded during the day
- **L** — Low: the lowest price traded during the day
- **C** — Close: the last transaction price of the day
- **V** — Volume: total shares traded

These five numbers are the raw material of every chart you'll ever see.

## Candlestick charts

A candlestick represents one period (a day, an hour, a minute). The body shows the range between open and close. The wicks show the high and low.

A **green candle** (or white): close was above open — buyers dominated.
A **red candle** (or black): close was below open — sellers dominated.

## Volume is the tell

Price moves without volume are suspect. If COMI moved 3% on twice the normal volume, something real happened. If it moved 3% on 20% of normal volume, be skeptical.

Volume is the most honest data you have. It's hard to fake.

## Common patterns worth knowing

**Support and resistance:** Price levels where buying or selling has historically concentrated. Not magic — just memory. The market remembers where it bounced before.

**Trend:** Is price making higher highs and higher lows (uptrend), lower highs and lower lows (downtrend), or neither (range)?

## What you should now understand

- OHLCV are the five numbers in every bar or candlestick
- Candlestick color tells you who won the day: buyers or sellers
- Volume confirms or questions price moves
- Support, resistance, and trend are the three basic concepts in chart reading
