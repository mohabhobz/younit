Egypt's financial markets aren't one thing. They're several parallel systems operating under different rules, serving different participants.

## The equity market: EGX

The Egyptian Exchange (EGX) lists equities and some structured products. It operates two indices you'll hear constantly:

**EGX 30** — The 30 most liquid stocks by market cap. This is the headline number. When people say "the market was up 1.2% today," they mean EGX 30.

**EGX 70** — A broader index of the next 70 stocks. Less watched but more representative of mid-cap Egypt.

Trading hours are Sunday through Thursday, 10:00–14:30 Cairo time.

## The regulator: FRA

The Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA) oversees capital markets in Egypt. It licenses brokers, sets disclosure rules, and investigates market abuse. Think of it as Egypt's SEC equivalent.

Every broker you'll use to access EGX data or trade is FRA-licensed.

## The infrastructure: Misr for Central Clearing

When you buy a share, the transaction doesn't settle instantly. In Egypt, equity trades settle T+2 — two business days after execution. The actual transfer of ownership happens through Misr for Central Clearing, Depository and Registry (MCDR).

## EFG Hermes in this picture

EFG Hermes is a licensed broker-dealer, investment bank, and asset manager. The API powering this platform draws from EFG's market data infrastructure — the same data that feeds EFG's own research operations.

## What you should now understand

- EGX is Egypt's equity market; EGX 30 is the benchmark index
- FRA is the regulator; MCDR handles settlement (T+2)
- Multiple asset classes trade in parallel: equities, bonds, currencies
- EFG Hermes is a licensed participant across all of these
