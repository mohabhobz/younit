A share price is an opinion. A financial statement is a record. Before buying, you want to understand whether the opinion (the price) is reasonable given the record (the business).

This session covers the three financial statements and the five ratios that matter most for EGX-listed companies.

## The three statements

**Income statement:** Revenue, costs, and what's left — profit. Shows how the business performed over a period. For a bank like CIB, the key line is Net Interest Income. For a manufacturer like EAST, it's Gross Margin.

**Balance sheet:** What the company owns (assets), what it owes (liabilities), and what's left for shareholders (equity). A snapshot at a point in time.

**Cash flow statement:** Where cash came from and where it went. Profit and cash flow diverge constantly due to accounting timing. The cash flow statement tells you what actually happened in cash terms.

## Five ratios worth knowing

**P/E (Price to Earnings):** What you're paying per pound of earnings. EGX 30 companies typically trade between 8x and 20x. Above 25x, you're paying for growth. Below 8x, the market is worried about something.

**P/B (Price to Book):** What you're paying per pound of net assets. Banks are frequently valued on P/B because their assets (loans) are their business.

**ROE (Return on Equity):** How efficiently the company generates profit from shareholder money. CIB has historically delivered 20%+ ROE — exceptional by regional standards.

**Debt/Equity:** How leveraged the balance sheet is. High leverage amplifies both gains and losses.

**EPS (Earnings per Share):** Net profit divided by shares outstanding. The base for P/E. Watch this trend over time, not just the current level.

## What you should now understand

- Three statements: income (performance), balance sheet (snapshot), cash flow (reality check)
- P/E and P/B are the two most common valuation multiples on EGX
- ROE tells you how efficiently a company uses equity capital
- Always read ratios in context: sector, history, and growth rate
