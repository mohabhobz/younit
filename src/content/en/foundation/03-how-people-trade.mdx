Trading sounds technical. The underlying acts are simple: you decide you want to own something, you communicate that decision to the market, the market finds someone with the opposite view, and you exchange.

## Two ways to say "I want to buy"

**Market order:** Buy immediately at whatever the current price is. Fast. Certain. You'll get filled. You won't control the price.

**Limit order:** Buy only if the price reaches a level I specify. You control the price. You might not get filled if the market never reaches your level.

Most sophisticated participants use limit orders. Market orders are for when speed matters more than price.

## The bid-ask spread

At any moment, the best price a buyer is willing to pay is the **bid**. The best price a seller is willing to accept is the **ask**. The difference is the **spread**.

On EGX, for a liquid stock like CIB, the spread might be EGP 0.05 on a EGP 50 stock — 0.1%. For an illiquid stock, it might be 1-2%. That spread is a real cost every time you trade.

## What happens after you click

1. Your order goes to your broker's system
2. The broker routes it to EGX's matching engine
3. The matching engine finds a counterpart order
4. The trade is reported; you see it in your portfolio
5. Two business days later (T+2), ownership formally transfers via MCDR

## Stop orders

A **stop order** triggers a market order when price reaches a specified level. Used for risk management: "if SWDY falls to EGP 14, sell immediately to limit my loss."

## What you should now understand

- Market orders guarantee execution but not price; limit orders guarantee price but not execution
- The bid-ask spread is a real transaction cost
- EGX settlement happens T+2 via MCDR
- Stop orders trigger market orders at a specified price level
