Your first share is more than a purchase; it’s your first step into the world of investing. It makes you a part-owner of a business, entitles you to a share of its profits, and puts your savings to work in an economy you already live in. This guide walks through the process from the ground up: what the Egyptian Exchange is, how it operates, how to open and fund an account, how to place an order, and what happens after you do. It assumes no prior knowledge. Read it end to end or jump to the step you need.

## What is API Trading

API trading lets software—not a human—interact directly with a broker or exchange. It can fetch real-time market data, place and manage orders, monitor accounts, run strategies automatically and execute trades programmatically.

## Key Capabilities

- Real-time data access (prices, order books, trade history).
- Automated order execution based on predefined logic.
- Backtesting using historical data.
- 24/7 operation without manual intervention.
- Deterministic workflows with structured request/response formats

## What a share is

A share is a unit of ownership in a company. When a company lists on a stock exchange, it divides ownership into shares and offers a portion to the public. Buying a share makes you a shareholder, a fractional owner with a claim on the company's assets and earnings.

Shareholders can make money in two ways:

- **Capital gains**, the share price rises above what you paid, and you sell at a profit.
- **Dividends**, the company distributes part of its profit to shareholders in cash.

Both depend on the company performing well and the market recognizing it. Neither is guaranteed. Share prices can fall or rise, and companies can reduce or suspend dividends.

## Why do companies list

Listing lets a company raise capital without borrowing. It sells ownership to investors, uses the proceeds to grow, and in return gives up a slice of future profits. For investors, listing creates a liquid market, a place where shares can be bought and sold at a transparent price during trading hours.

## Why people invest in shares

Cash loses purchasing power over time. Shares are one of the few assets that can outpace inflation over long periods, because they represent ownership of businesses that raise prices, expand, and compound earnings. That potential comes with volatility. The trade-off is straightforward: higher expected returns in exchange for accepting that the value of your holdings will fluctuate, sometimes sharply, along the way.
