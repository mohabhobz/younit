## History and structure

The Egyptian Exchange (EGX) is among the oldest in the region. The Alexandria Exchange was established in 1883 and the Cairo Exchange in 1903; the two were later unified into a single national exchange. Today the EGX operates a Main Market for established companies and a market for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), alongside listings for bonds and other instruments.

## Who oversees the market?

Three institutions matter to a retail investor:

- **The Egyptian Exchange (EGX)** operates the trading venue, sets listing and trading rules, and publishes prices and indices.
- **The Financial Regulatory Authority (FRA)**, the government regulator for non-bank financial services, including capital markets. It licenses brokers, supervises listed companies' disclosures, and enforces investor-protection rules.
- **Misr for Central Clearing, Depository and Registry (MCDR)** holds share ownership records electronically and settles trades. When you buy shares, MCDR records them in your name, not physically.

MCDR assigns every investor on the EGX a unified investor code. Your broker applies for it when you open an account. It is the identifier under which all your holdings are registered, regardless of which broker you use.

## The indices

An index is a basket of shares used to measure the market's overall direction. The ones you will hear about most:

- **EGX 30**, the 30 most actively traded companies on the exchange, weighted by market capitalization. This is the headline benchmark; when the news says, "the market rose today," it usually means the EGX 30.
- **EGX 70 EWI**, 70 companies outside the EGX 30, equally weighted. Often used as a proxy for mid- and small-cap performance.
- **EGX 100 EWI**, the EGX 30 and EGX 70 combined.
- **EGX 33 Shariah**, companies that meet Shariah-compliance screening.

An index is a reference, not something you buy directly. But it tells you whether the tide is rising or falling.

## When the market is open

The EGX trades from **Sunday to Thursday**. Friday and Saturday are closed, as are Egyptian public holidays.

The standard trading day, in Cairo time:

- **9:30–10:00**, pre-opening session. You can enter, change, or cancel orders, but nothing is executed. An auction sets the opening price at 10:00.
- **10:00–14:30**, continuous trading. Orders match in real time.
- **Closing auction**: a short auction near the end of the session establishes the official closing price.

During Ramadan, the session is shortened, typically running from 10:00 to 13:30. The exchange announces the adjusted schedule each year.

## Currency and sectors

The majority of the EGX-listed shares are trading in Egyptian pounds, except for few companies/banks. The market's weight is concentrated in banking, real estate, telecommunications, food and beverages, basic materials, non-bank financial services, and industrials, sectors that track the domestic economy closely.
