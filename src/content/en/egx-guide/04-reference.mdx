## Glossary

- **Bid** — the highest price a buyer is currently willing to pay.
- **Ask (Offer)** — the lowest price a seller is currently willing to accept.
- **Blue chip** — a large, established company with a long record of stable earnings.
- **Bull market / Bear market** — a sustained period of rising prices / a sustained period of falling prices.
- **Closing price** — the official price at the end of the trading session, set by the closing auction.
- **Dividend** — a distribution of company profit to shareholders, is usually in cash.
- **Ex-dividend date** — the first day a share trades without entitlement to the upcoming dividend.
- **Free float** — the portion of a company's shares available for public trading.
- **IPO (Initial Public Offering)** — a company's first sale of shares to the public.
- **Limit order** — an order to buy or sell at a specified price or better.
- **Liquidity** — how easily a share can be bought or sold without moving its price.
- **Market order** — an order to buy or sell immediately at the best available price.
- **Portfolio** — the full set of investments you hold.
- **Record date** — the date on which you must be a registered shareholder to receive a dividend or participate in a corporate action.
- **Rights issue** — an offer to existing shareholders to buy new shares, usually at a discount, in proportion to their holding.
- **Settlement** — the transfer of shares and cash that completes a trade, two business days after execution on the EGX.
- **Spread** — the difference between the bid and ask prices.
- **Stock split** — dividing each existing share into several, lowering the price per share without changing the company's value.
- **Unified investor code** — your unique identifier at MCDR under which all your EGX holdings are registered.
- **Volatility** — the degree to which a share's price moves over time.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much money do I need to start?

Enough to buy one share of the company you have chosen, plus commission. There is no regulatory minimum. Start with an amount you are comfortable seeing fall by a quarter without changing your plans.

### Can I lose more than I invest?

Not when buying shares outright. Your maximum loss is what you paid. Leverage and margin trading change this, which is why they are not for beginners.

### How long does account opening take?

Digital onboarding on EFG Hermes ONE is designed to be completed in a single session. Approval follows verification of your documents.

### Do I need to be an Egyptian citizen?

Non-residents and foreign nationals can invest on the EGX, subject to their own KYC requirements and a different tax treatment. This guide is written for Egyptian residents.

### Are my shares safe if my broker fails?

Your shares are registered in your name at MCDR, not held by the broker. Broker failure does not affect ownership of your securities.

### What is the difference between investing and trading?

Investing means buying shares to hold for years, based on the value of the underlying business. Trading means buying and selling over days or weeks, based on price movements. Both are legitimate; they require different skills, and trading is far harder to do profitably than it looks.

### Should I buy shares or a mutual fund?

A fund gives you diversification in a single purchase and delegates stock selection to a professional manager, for a fee. Direct shares give you control and lower ongoing costs but require your own research. Many investors hold both. EFG Hermes ONE offers access to both, including gold, USD fixed income, money market, equity, and Shariah-compliant funds.
