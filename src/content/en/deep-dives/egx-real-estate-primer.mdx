Real estate companies account for a meaningful slice of EGX market cap, yet they're frequently misread by analysts applying industrial-company frameworks to a fundamentally different business model.

The Egyptian listed real estate sector includes TALAT (Talaat Moustafa Group), OCDI (Orascom Development), PHD (Palm Hills Development), and MNHD (Madinet Nasr Housing), among others. Each operates differently, but they share one characteristic: the land bank is the business.

## Why NAV matters more than P/E

For most companies, you start with earnings. For Egyptian real estate developers, you start with the land bank — what they own, what it's worth at today's prices, and how many years of development it represents.

Net Asset Value (NAV) is the sum of the land bank at current market value, plus completed and under-construction assets, minus net debt. Developers trade at discounts or premiums to NAV depending on execution track record, delivery speed, and the credibility of management.

A company trading at 0.6x NAV is either cheap or has an execution problem. The market is asking you to figure out which.

## The delivery cycle risk

Egyptian real estate developers typically sell off-plan — units sold before they're built. Revenue is recognized as construction progresses. Cash comes in early; costs come later. This creates a structural mismatch that can look good in good times and dangerous in bad times.

When interest rates rise sharply (as they did in 2024-2025), the cost of carry on unsold inventory and the slowdown in new sales can hit simultaneously. The companies with the strongest balance sheets — lowest net debt relative to NAV — survive this best.

## Metrics to watch

- **NAV per share** and the discount/premium to current price
- **Net debt / total assets** — leverage tells you how much cushion exists
- **Contracted sales** — forward indicator of revenue recognition
- **Delivery rate** — are they building as fast as they're selling?
- **Geographic concentration** — Talaat Moustafa is heavily concentrated in New Cairo/Madinaty; this is both an advantage (brand) and a risk (single market)
