What becomes newly possible — and why AI comes after rules, testing, risk, and architecture.

01 — WHY AI COMES LAST

AI Is Not the Starting Point

The first six parts deliberately avoided AI. That was not because AI is irrelevant. It was because AI is powerful only after the foundation is clear.

A beginner often hears “AI trading” and imagines a system that predicts the market on its own. That is the wrong frame. Before AI can be useful, you still need the basics: clear rules, clean data, backtesting discipline, live risk controls, and an architecture that can be monitored.

AI does not remove those requirements. It sits on top of them. It helps the system read more, adapt more, and learn from changing conditions — but it does not replace the need to know what the system is doing.

The progression is still:
rules-based algo first → AI-augmented algo later.
02 — THE DISTINCTION

Rules-Based vs AI-Augmented

The words are often mixed together. They should not be.

Rules-Based Algo

A human defines the rules. The system follows them consistently: when to enter, when to exit, how much to size, and when to stop.


Strength: clear, testable, explainable, easier to debug.

AI-Augmented Algo

AI is added to a rules-based system to read unstructured information, adjust behavior, or update parameters as conditions change.


Strength: broader inputs, more adaptability, better handling of messy information.

↳ AI is not magic dust sprinkled on bad rules. Bad rules with AI are still bad rules — just harder to understand.
03 — THREE CAPABILITIES

What AI Actually Adds

For this beginner track, keep it to three conceptual capabilities. Anything deeper belongs in future deep dives.

Capability 01

Reading Unstructured Data

AI can process news, filings, transcripts, social sentiment, and text that traditional rules cannot easily convert into signals.
Capability 02

Adaptive Strategy Behavior

Instead of using fixed parameters forever, AI can help a system adjust how it behaves when market conditions change.
Capability 03

Retraining Against Decay

As edges weaken, models can be retrained on newer data so the system does not stay frozen in an old market regime.

These are meaningful capabilities. They are also not shortcuts. Each one creates new testing, data, and monitoring problems. The more adaptive a system becomes, the more careful you need to be about how you validate it.

04 — NEWS AND TEXT

Sentiment and Unstructured Data

Traditional algos are good with numbers. AI makes text usable.

A rules-based system can easily read price, volume, moving averages, volatility, and balance sheet fields. It struggles with messy text: breaking news, management commentary, regulatory filings, social posts, analyst notes, and market rumors.

AI changes that. It can classify whether a news item is positive or negative, identify which company it affects, summarize an earnings release, detect a regulatory event, or flag a sudden shift in sentiment.

That last point matters. You often do not have perfect historical news data at the same granularity as price data. You may know that a major event happened, but not every small headline the market saw in real time. So AI sentiment can be powerful live, but difficult to backtest cleanly unless you build the dataset yourself over time.

05 — ADAPTATION

Adaptive Strategies

Traditional algos are usually static. AI can help systems adjust as the market changes.

A simple moving-average strategy might use the same 20-day and 50-day windows forever. That is clear and testable, but it assumes the same settings remain useful across different regimes. In reality, markets change. Volatility changes. Liquidity changes. Crowded trades decay.

AI can help by adjusting parameters, weighting signals differently, or learning which conditions favor which behavior. Reinforcement learning is one version of this idea: the system learns from actions and outcomes over time.

Static Strategy

Rules and parameters stay fixed unless a human changes them. Easier to understand, test, and debug.

Adaptive Strategy

The system can update behavior as conditions change. More flexible, but also harder to validate and monitor.

Adaptation is powerful, but dangerous if you cannot test it.

A model that constantly changes can also constantly overfit.
06 — ALPHA DECAY

Retraining as a Defense Against Decay

Part 5 introduced alpha decay: the slow erosion of an edge as markets and traders adapt. AI gives one possible response.

A static strategy can remain frozen around the market it was built for. That may work for a while. But if the market changes, or if other traders crowd into the same opportunity, the edge can weaken. The strategy may still run perfectly while its returns quietly degrade.

Retraining is the idea of periodically feeding the model newer data and updating how it behaves. This might happen every few weeks, months, or quarters depending on the strategy and market. The goal is not to chase every recent move. The goal is to stop the system from being trapped in an old version of the market.

07 — SYSTEM DESIGN

Where AI Fits in the Architecture

AI should be a component inside the system, not an excuse to make the system unexplainable.

In the Part 6 architecture, AI can live in several places. It might support the Research Agent by turning news into signals. It might support the Portfolio Manager by estimating changing volatility. It might support monitoring by flagging unusual behavior. But it should not bypass risk.

A1

AI Inside Research

Reads text, classifies sentiment, detects events, or generates additional signal inputs. The final trade still passes through sizing, risk, and execution.

A2

AI Inside Monitoring

Flags unusual live behavior: fills worse than expected, signals firing too often, drawdown patterns changing, or data behaving strangely.

A3

AI Inside Adaptation

Updates parameters or strategy behavior over time, but only through a controlled retraining and validation process.

AI can suggest, classify, adapt, and monitor.

But the Risk Officer still outranks it.
08 — WHAT NOT TO DO

Do Not Use AI to Hide Vague Thinking

The fastest way to build a system you cannot trust is to let AI replace clarity.

  • Do not use AI because you do not have a clear strategy.
  • Do not let a model make live decisions you cannot monitor or limit.
  • Do not retrain on recent data and call it improvement without a clean holdout test.
  • Do not let AI bypass position sizing, exposure limits, drawdown limits, or kill switches.
  • Do not confuse complexity with edge.

The best AI systems in trading are not mysterious black boxes floating outside the architecture. They are components in a disciplined system. They have inputs, outputs, limits, logs, and failure modes.

10 — SUMMARY

What You Should Now Understand

The final takeaways from the Algo Track.

Key Takeaways — Part 07
  • AI trading is best understood as AI-augmented algo trading, not a replacement for rules-based thinking.
  • AI adds three major capabilities: reading unstructured data, adapting strategy behavior, and retraining against alpha decay.
  • Sentiment and news signals are powerful, but serious use requires clean timestamped data collected over time.
  • Adaptive strategies can help with changing markets, but they are harder to validate and easier to overfit.
  • AI should live inside the architecture. It should never bypass sizing, risk controls, monitoring, or kill switches.
  • The foundation remains the same: rules, testing, live controls, architecture, and disciplined supervision.
