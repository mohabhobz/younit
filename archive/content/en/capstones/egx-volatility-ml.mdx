## Abstract

Standard GARCH models perform well for Egyptian market volatility during calm periods but break down during geopolitical shocks — the 2022 FX crisis, the 2023 rate shock — where volatility regimes shift abruptly. This project tests whether gradient-boosted tree models with lagged sentiment features can improve on GARCH-based forecasts across different market regimes.

## What we built

A LightGBM ensemble trained on five years of EGX 30 data with the following feature set:
- Lagged realized volatility (1, 5, 21-day windows)
- OHLCV-derived technical indicators (ATR, RSI, Bollinger band width)
- Arabic news sentiment scores from the Hub's sentiment API
- Macro features: USD/EGP rate, 10Y T-bill yield, overnight rate

## Results

Against a GARCH(1,1) benchmark:
- Calm periods (2021–2023): roughly equivalent RMSE
- High-volatility periods (2023–2025): LightGBM RMSE 18% lower
- The sentiment feature added 4% improvement; macro features added 6%

The model is not production-ready — it hasn't been through the full validation pipeline needed for a live risk system. But the research result is credible.

## What we'd do next

Extend to individual EGX 30 constituents. The challenge is sparse data for smaller names. Also explore LSTM-based approaches for potentially capturing longer memory in the volatility series.
