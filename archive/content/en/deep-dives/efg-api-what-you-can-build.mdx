The EFG API is a market data API, not a trading API. You can read prices, historical data, fundamentals, and corporate actions. You cannot submit orders, manage positions, or access custody accounts through it.

This distinction matters because it defines what you're building: analysis tools, screeners, educational apps, research infrastructure. Not execution systems.

## What the API provides

**Price data.** End-of-day OHLCV for all EGX-listed securities, going back several years. Adjusted for splits and dividends. Intraday data (1-minute bars) is available for the current and prior 30 trading days.

**Fundamentals.** Quarterly and annual financial statements for listed companies, as reported to EGX. Income statement, balance sheet, cash flow. Coverage is comprehensive for EGX 30 constituents; thinner for small-caps.

**Corporate actions.** Dividend announcements, stock splits, rights issues, delistings. Critical for backtesting — you need these to build accurate price series.

**Index data.** EGX 30 and EGX 70 constituents and weights, updated daily. Historical constituent changes are available, which matters for avoiding survivorship bias in backtests.

**Screener endpoints.** Filter securities by financial metrics — P/E range, market cap, sector, liquidity threshold. Returns a list of tickers matching your criteria at a given date.

## What it doesn't provide

- Real-time data (end-of-day only for historical; 15-minute delay for intraday)
- Order book data (level 2)
- Options or derivatives data
- Macro data (interest rates, FX, inflation) — use public sources for these
- Sentiment or news data — this is what builders are adding on top

## What people are actually building

Looking at the showcase projects, a pattern emerges. The most useful applications fall into three categories:

**Screeners.** Filter the EGX universe by fundamental or technical criteria. Screen for low P/E with high ROE. Screen for stocks near 52-week lows with rising volume. The API's screener endpoint makes this tractable.

**Analytics tools.** Deeper analysis of individual securities or portfolios. Risk metrics, attribution analysis, drawdown analysis. Tools that answer specific questions rather than scan the whole universe.

**Educational tools.** Applications that help users understand market data — visualizing how P/E has changed over time for a specific company, showing how a portfolio would have performed, explaining what a financial statement means.

The showcase section has working examples of all three.
